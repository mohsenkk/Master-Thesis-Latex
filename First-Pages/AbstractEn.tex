	\textbf{\large \textcolor{black}{Abstract} }
\end{center}


\textcolor{black}{
Drug–Target Binding Affinity (DTA) prediction is an important problem in computational drug discovery, aiming to quantitatively estimate the binding strength between a drug compound and a target protein. The high cost and time requirements of experimental approaches, together with the large space of potential compounds and biological targets, have motivated the use of machine learning and deep learning methods as complementary approaches to this problem.
This thesis investigates deep learning methods for drug–target binding affinity prediction, with a particular focus on generative models and representation learning. In this context, the representation of drugs and proteins, model architectures, training procedures, and evaluation methods are studied. Two generative approaches based on Generative Adversarial Networks and Variational Autoencoders are analyzed and compared through the DCGAN-DTA and Co-VAE models, respectively.
A main objective of this study is to systematically examine the reproducibility and fair comparability of these approaches. To this end, data preprocessing, dataset splitting, model architectures, training parameters, and evaluation procedures are carefully investigated and documented. The models are also evaluated and compared on commonly used DTA benchmark datasets, including Davis and KIBA. Finally, the strengths and limitations of the investigated approaches are analyzed in terms of representation quality, predictive performance, training complexity, and reproducibility, providing a basis for the development of more effective methods for drug–target binding affinity prediction.
}
